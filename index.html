When the shelf goes empty, customers keep asking. Sales data stops listening.

A sold-out item records zero sales, not zero demand. Forecasts trained on those records order less next time, so the shelf empties earlier. We estimate the demand that stockouts hide, from sales patterns and from app signals, and measure what it does to orders.

Empty shelves pile up while people are still shopping

Across 898 real fresh-grocery stores in FreshRetailNet-50K, 44% of store-item-days run out of stock during opening hours, and most are not restocked that day.

The share of items out of stock climbs from about 7% in the morning to about 40% by 9 pm, while customers keep ordering through the evening.

How it works

Two independent estimates of what customers wanted during a sell-out, combined, then turned into tomorrow's order.

From sales history

The item's usual intra-day pattern, plus a model trained on synthetic sell-outs generated the way real ones happen: when demand passes the stock on hand.

From app signals

Customers still open a sold-out product page. Unique viewers during the sell-out × the item's usual view-to-purchase rate estimates demand directly.

Combine and forecast

Each estimate is weighted by its uncertainty. Next-day forecasts are trained on recovered demand instead of recorded sales.

Order

A newsvendor rule turns the forecast into an order at the chosen service level, netting out stock still on hand.

How we test it

Rolling time splits; a check on real sell-out days that needs no assumptions; and a simulated store where true demand is known on every day, so orders can be scored against the truth.

Real data

Recorded sales guarantee stockouts

On a day that sold out, demand was at least what sold. An order below that is a guaranteed stockout. Forecasting from recovered demand cuts these days from 60% to 39%.

Simulated store

Recovered demand reaches the perfect-information trade-off

Forecasting from recorded sales cannot reach an 80% fill rate at any setting. Recovered demand, especially from app signals, sits on the oracle curve.

Real sell-outs happen on high-demand days

With known demand, sales-only recovery under-estimates what was lost. App signals are nearly unbiased.

What is real and what is simulated

Real data

Sales-history recovery, forecasts and the sell-out-day check use FreshRetailNet-50K (anonymised, normalised sales). The app-signal results use REES46 product-page views and purchases (general e-commerce, not grocery).

Simulated

The store with known true demand, and app views on grocery demand. Both are built on real demand patterns and labelled wherever shown. The method transfers; exact percentages will differ on other data.
